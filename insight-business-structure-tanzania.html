Choosing the right business structure in Tanzania

Sole proprietorship, partnership, limited company or branch? How each option affects your risk, tax and paperwork.

By the STAA teamOctober 20262 min read

The legal structure you choose affects your personal risk, how you’re taxed, how easily you can raise money and how much paperwork you’ll have. Here are the main options in Tanzania and the questions to ask before you decide.

The main options

Sole proprietorship

You trade in your own name or under a business name registered with BRELA. It’s simple and cheap to set up, and profits are taxed as your personal income. The downside: there’s no legal separation between you and the business, so you’re personally liable for its debts.

Partnership

Two or more people share ownership and profits. A written partnership agreement is essential. It should cover contributions, profit sharing and what happens if a partner leaves. As with a sole proprietorship, partners are generally personally liable.

Private limited company

A company incorporated under the Companies Act and registered with BRELA is a separate legal person. Shareholders’ liability is generally limited to their investment. Companies pay corporate income tax on profits, and dividends to shareholders are taxed separately. There are more compliance obligations: annual returns, statutory records and audited financial statements.

Branch of a foreign company

Foreign businesses can register a branch in Tanzania instead of a local subsidiary. A branch isn’t a separate legal entity from its parent, and it has its own tax rules, so compare it carefully with setting up a local company.

Questions to ask before you choose

  • Risk: how much personal liability are you willing to carry?
  • Growth: will you need investors or bank finance? Most prefer to deal with a company.
  • Tax: how will profits be taxed, and how will you take money out of the business?
  • Ownership: will there be several owners now or later?
  • Contracts: do your customers or tenders require a registered company?
  • Admin: can you keep up with the ongoing filing and audit requirements?

After registration

Whatever structure you choose, you’ll also need a TIN from TRA, the right business licence, and possibly VAT registration and employer registrations once you take on staff. Building these into your plan from the start avoids penalties later.

The bottom line

There’s no single right answer. A sole proprietorship can be ideal for testing an idea, while a limited company usually suits a business that’s growing, hiring or raising finance. Get advice on both the legal and tax sides before you register, since changing structure later costs more.

This article is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules change and every business is different, so speak to an STAA advisor about your situation.
